## Research Overview

The project explores how artificial intelligence can support personalized healthcare by combining longitudinal clinical information with patient-generated, genomic, laboratory, and wearable data.

The research focuses on five major areas:

1. Personalized healthcare
2. Clinical decision support
3. Healthcare interoperability
4. Healthcare AI security and privacy
5. Regulatory and ethical considerations

## Personalized Healthcare

Traditional healthcare systems may contain patient information across multiple disconnected sources. A personalized medical assistant could provide a consolidated view of relevant patient information and use AI-based analysis to identify patterns and potential risks.

## Clinical Decision Support

AI can support clinicians by assisting with medication reconciliation, risk assessment, diagnostic support, personalized care planning, and pre-visit preparation.

The system is designed as decision support rather than an autonomous replacement for clinical judgment.

## Genomic and Pharmacogenomic Support

Genomic information can potentially be incorporated into clinical decision support to identify genetic variants, pharmacogenomic information, hereditary risk, and possible drug-gene interactions.

Potential data resources include ClinVar, dbSNP, and PharmGKB.

## Healthcare Interoperability

HL7 FHIR and SMART-on-FHIR provide the proposed interoperability foundation, allowing healthcare information to be exchanged between systems using standardized APIs and data structures.

## AI Technologies

The proposed architecture considers:

- Machine learning
- Predictive analytics
- NLP
- Large language models
- Clinical decision rules
- Explainable AI

Explainability techniques such as SHAP and LIME can be considered for providing insight into model outputs.

## Privacy and Security

Healthcare information requires strong privacy and security controls. The proposed architecture considers encryption, TLS, RBAC, MFA, audit logging, consent management, data minimization, de-identification, and security monitoring.

## Regulatory and Ethical Considerations

The research also considers:

- HIPAA
- GDPR
- FDA requirements for medical software
- EU MDR / IVDR
- EU AI Act
- Algorithmic bias
- Human oversight
- Health equity
- Data governance
